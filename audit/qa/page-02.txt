We therefore study a deliberately explicit experiment: independent isotropic Gaussian designs,
independent Gaussian regression errors, and a fixed-dimensional coefficient in a bounded TT
rank class or a known neighborhood of that class. The main length estimand is a prespecified
linear functional of the coefficient, not an individual TT core, a training-trajectory statistic, or an
unobserved neural-network parameterization. Our main objective is to classify the best expected
interval length at rank-one coefficients subject to coverage for every coefficient in the declared
class. We allow unknown error variance but do not claim inference when ambient least squares is
unavailable.
Three obstacles distinguish the calculation from a direct delta-method exercise. First, a path in
an enclosing product model may violate an internal TT rank cap, so a lower modulus bound requires
an actually feasible tensor path. Second, a confidence interval calibrated at the exact boundary
can fail under statistically indistinguishable local changes, as Figure 1 illustrates. Third, the fitted
loading projections have lower-order components even when the true ones vanish; these terms and
the event of poor pilot localization must be controlled to bound expected length.
The construction uses the cuts whose rank cap equals one to partition the tensor chain. Orthog-
onal loading components are graded by the number of blocks perpendicular to the signal. For a
fixed loading, the smallest nonzero degree determines the modulus exponent. Retaining all degrees
gives a uniform formula when low-degree components approach zero. Within-block sums of two
elementary products provide feasible witness paths despite the remaining TT constraints. Averaging
signed paths isolates each degree without cancellation from lower ones. Confidence-ball inversion
then supplies global coverage, while a graded product bound makes the calculation explicit. In the
four-way example this recovers cubic inference. A six-way example with a proper singular internal
rank constraint has the same cubic order, demonstrating both the scope of the classification and
the limited role of that singularity.
The paper makes three claims, each proved in the appendix:
1. Section 4 classifies local contact and optimal honest expected length at rank-one signals for
arbitrary positive TT cap profiles, uniformly over deterministic loadings. It also shows that
slack at every cut restores first-order contact for every nonzero loading.
2. Theorem 3 determines the additional length required by a known structural approximation
allowance and identifies departures that cannot be detected but invalidate exact-model lengths.
3. Section 5 gives a computable, globally honest interval that attains these orders without
selecting the contact order. The improved energy certificate is no longer than the original on
any dataset. Section 6 separates localization assumptions from geometry; experiments test
the rate transitions, coverage distinction, and approximation sensitivity.

Related work and the scope of the contribution
Degenerate functionals and singular tests.
Chen and Fang (2019) develops second-order
functional inference and explains ordinary-bootstrap failure; higher-order extensions are explicitly
discussed. Dufour et al. (2025) studies locally singular polynomial Wald restrictions. Pillai and
Meng (2016) gives the exact Gaussian monomial Wald law, including the cubic χ2
1/9 case. Bei and
Navjeevan (2026) constructs uniformly valid minimum-distance inference under degeneracy and
discusses extensions beyond a nonsingular Hessian. These results preclude novelty claims based
solely on a cubic delta method or a nonstandard Wald limit. Our explicit local Wald calculation is
a supporting specialization of that literature.
