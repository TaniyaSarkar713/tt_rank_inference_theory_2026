Contact Orders and the Fragility of Honest Tensor Inference
Research draft for author review
8 September 2026: audited revision
Abstract
A linear functional of a tensor coefficient can have several vanishing derivatives on a rank-
constrained model. We study globally honest inference in Gaussian tensor regression with fixed
ambient dimension. At original-mode rank-one signals, cuts with tensor-train rank cap one
partition the modes into blocks. We prove a local modulus formula that retains every orthogonal
loading degree, uniformly over loadings and signal directions. The resulting optimal pointwise
expected interval length describes transitions between regular and degenerate functionals, in-
cluding loadings that vary with sample size. For a fixed loading of first nonzero degree q, the
scale reduces to (σ/√n)q/λq−1, with radial degree treated as first order. A known coefficient
neighborhood adds ∥A∥min{η, σ/√n}. A computable HOSVD confidence-ball certificate attains
these orders, with an exact unknown-variance version and simultaneous coverage over contrasts.
An energy refinement shortens the original certificate on every dataset. We separate geometric
inference from localization, extending the analysis to quantitative non-Gaussian design conditions
and retaining the full contact rate with a median-based localizer under independent observations
and a known conditional variance bound. Simulations approach the predicted exponents and
illustrate local Wald failure; a separate paired study confirms the certificate improvement while
documenting remaining conservatism. The contribution is the constrained tensor modulus
and its inferential consequences, rather than generic higher-order inference or a new modulus
lower-bound principle.
Keywords: confidence intervals; tensor train; local modulus; singular inference; model approxima-
tion; higher-order contact.

Introduction
Structural restrictions can reduce uncertainty about a regression functional by more than a variance
constant. Consider a four-way coefficient B ∈R2×2×2×2 whose TT rank caps are (1, 2, 1). The outer
rank-one cuts force a product of blocks of dimensions (2, 4, 2). At B0 = λE0000, the coordinate
functional B 7→B1111 changes only when all three blocks change. A perturbation of Frobenius
size t can change this functional by order t3/λ2. This raises a statistical question: can an interval
exploit that cubic behavior while retaining coverage over the whole rank-constrained class, including
neighboring rank strata?
Existing theory makes a simple higher-order calculation insufficient as a contribution. Functional
inference under degeneracy, including corrected bootstrap and minimum-distance methods, is well
developed (Chen and Fang, 2019; Bei and Navjeevan, 2026). Tensor inference with nondegenerate
tangent alignment also has strong guarantees (Xu et al., 2025; Ma and Xia, 2024). What requires a
separate calculation here is the local modulus across a specified collection of TT constraints and the
corresponding expected length under global honesty. In addition, structural rank restrictions may
be approximate, and a small coefficient departure can be larger than a higher-order inferential scale.
