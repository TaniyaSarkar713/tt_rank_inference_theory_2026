7.2
Contact orders and internal singularity
We take B0 = 3E00···0, A = E11···1, and σ = 0.2. Three binary modes with caps (1, 1) give q = 3.
Four binary modes with caps (1, 1, 1), (1, 2, 1), (2, 1, 2), and (2, 2, 2) give q = 4, 3, 2, 1, respectively.
Six binary modes with caps (1, 2, 2, 2, 1) give the internally singular q = 3 example. The primary
sample sizes are 512, 2048 and 8192.
The mean-length slopes approach the predicted −q/2 exponents; Table 1 reports both the
primary and larger-n estimates. The primary six-mode slope is −1.776, so those sample sizes alone
should not be described as a precise confirmation of −1.5. The addendum uses n = 131072 and
2097152, with unchanged methods and tuning. Its six-mode slope is −1.511, while the four-way
product slope is −2.010. Monte Carlo errors in the table describe uncertainty in simulated means,
not error from approximating an asymptotic exponent.
