Moduli, curved models, and model neighborhoods.
The connection between honest interval
length and between-class moduli is central to Cai and Low (2004). Its lower-bound logic applies
to singleton evaluation classes and does not require a new tensor information inequality. Andrews
and Mikusheva (2016) already obtains conservative inference from curved-model geometry. Model-
misspecification sensitivity is studied by Bonhomme and Weidner (2022), and bias-aware intervals
and efficiency comparisons by Armstrong and Koles´ar (2021); Armstrong et al. (2023).
Our
neighborhood calculation uses the elementary modulus sandwich; the specific input is the classified
TT contact order. We do not claim a new general theory of robust confidence intervals.
Rank uncertainty and tensor inference.
Carpentier et al. (2018) shows that rank-adaptive
matrix confidence sets can exist, with feasibility depending on the observation and noise experiment.
Armstrong et al. (2026) allows weak factors in panel inference. Thus rank uncertainty is not a
universal impossibility mechanism. In regular tensor settings, Xu et al. (2025) and Ma and Xia
(2024) obtain functional Gaussian limits under signal, initialization, and nondegenerate alignment
conditions. Their dimensions can increase, and their regular-regime results are stronger than the
fixed-dimensional analysis here in that respect. We address a different boundary regime rather than
improving their sample complexity. Xia et al. (2022) also establishes important tensor inference
results without a universal need for debiasing.
