Discussion and limitations
The main conclusion is a loading-uniform local modulus and honest-length formula at original-
mode rank-one points in capped TT classes.
It retains all orthogonal degrees and therefore
describes transitions that an integer contact order alone cannot represent. Cap-one cuts determine
the exponent, while internal constraints can leave a singular feasible set without changing that
exponent. If every cut permits an additional rank, all nonzero linear functionals return to first
order. These statements describe the geometry of the declared class, not a universal benefit of
tensor parameterization or rank selection.
The approximation result is consequential for interpretation. A confidence interval with an
n−3/2 pointwise length relies on a structural allowance of the same order if that gain is to persist
uniformly. An ambient coefficient departure of order between n−3/2 and n−1/2 can be statistically
undetectable and still dominate the target uncertainty. The theorem therefore argues for explicit
sensitivity analysis whenever rank restrictions are approximate. It does not provide a way to infer
the required small allowance from the same sample without additional assumptions.
The limitations are substantial. The pointwise length theorem fixes ambient dimension and uses
an original-mode rank-one evaluation point; its constants are uniform over the unit signal directions
and deterministic loadings. The OLS ball can incur large dimension constants. Although the energy
refinement substantially shortens intervals at a tested cubic point, the experiments still show that
it is far from a sharp constant procedure. No matching dimension-dependent minimax length,
efficient confidence constant, or general classification at non-rank-one TT points has been established.
Coverage for data-selected contrasts follows from the common ball, but the expected-length formula
for arbitrary dependent loadings and coverage conditional on selection remain separate questions.
The local norm-hardness result does not establish hardness of optimal inference.
