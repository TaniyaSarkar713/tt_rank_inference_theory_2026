Counterexamples and boundaries of the conclusions
These examples test proposed relaxations. They do not contradict the stated theorems.
C1: zero signal changes the order.
At B0 = 0 any elementary rank-one H gives a feasible path
sH under every positive cap profile. For nonzero A, choose H with ⟨A, H⟩̸= 0. Then ωA,0(t; 0) ≍t,
including classes with several cap-one cuts. Thus the order tq/λq−1 cannot be continued through
λ = 0. More generally ω ≤∥A∥t, so a higher-order formula cannot hold without a local restriction
on t/λ.
C2: uncertain validity of cap-one restrictions.
In four binary modes, B0 = λE0000 and
A = E1111 have cubic contact under caps (1, 2, 1). If the honesty class instead permits caps (2, 2, 2),
the path B0 +sE1111 is feasible and changes the target by s. The root-n two-point lower bound then
applies. Choosing the smaller caps from the same data does not remove the larger-class alternatives.
C3: an unobserved structured direction.
For a two-mode coefficient with cap one, consider
Bθ = θE11 and a design satisfying Xi,11 = 0 almost surely. Distinct θ produce exactly the same
data distribution, although all coefficients in this submodel have rank at most one. The coordinate
functional is not identifiable. Thus full ambient identifiability can be weakened to injectivity on
structured differences, but no such injectivity assumption can simply be omitted. This example
does not assert impossibility for every structured model with p ≥n.
C4: independent-error scaling cannot cover arbitrary dependence.
Take scalar regression
with Xi = 1 and errors εi = Z for all i, where Z ∼N(0, σ2). Then z −b = Z does not shrink,
whereas residual variance is zero. The residual-F interval is the singleton {z} and has zero coverage
for b. This example embeds in K ≥2 by taking all mode dimensions one. Its error covariance has
largest eigenvalue nσ2, violating the bounded conditional covariance assumption in Proposition 10.
C5: finite variance does not give the Gaussian pivot.
For p = 1, n = 2 and fixed X = (1, 0)⊤,
let the first error be zero with probability 0.9 and equal to ±

10σ with probability 0.05 each;
take the second error independently with the same distribution. These errors have mean zero and
variance σ2. The known-Gaussian 95% radius is 1.96σ and its coverage is 0.9. The variance-bound
radius in Proposition 10 avoids this failure. This is a finite-sample pivot counterexample, not a
claim that asymptotic Gaussian approximation is impossible under finite variance.
