7.5
Unknown noise, dense signals, and assumption diagnostics
The study also varies λ ∈{0.5, 1, 3}, rotates the signal and loading densely within modes, uses
Gaussian design covariance Σij = 0.5|i−j|, and evaluates the residual-F procedure. The latter retains
exact conditional coverage under Gaussian homoskedastic errors; a correlated full-rank design does
not invalidate that statement. Dense rotations illustrate that coefficient sparsity is not needed.
Separate stress cases use standardized t5 errors, heteroskedastic Gaussian errors, AR(1) errors
with correlation 0.6, and Gaussian scale mixtures with 1% or 5% outlier probability and outlier
standard deviation 5σ. These cases are outside the exact noise theorem. For instance, at 5%
contamination the ambient interval using the uncontaminated σ covers only 0.8045, while its
residual-t version covers 0.947 in this experiment. Conservative graded coverage at the tested
boundary does not establish robustness to any of these departures. All diagnostic results, rather
than only favorable settings, are included in the companion tables.
