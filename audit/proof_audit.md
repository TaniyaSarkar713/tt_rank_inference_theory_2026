# Adversarial proof audit

Scope: all 11 numbered baseline results, the inline geometric/statistical assertions listed in `theorem_inventory.md`, and every result added by this audit. Complete revised proofs are in `paper/appendix.tex`, `paper/appendix_strengthening.tex`, and `paper/appendix_counterexamples.tex`; this document records independent checks and failure points. VERIFIED means checked internal derivation, not formal proof verification or external peer review.

## 1. Geometry reconstructed without a tangent-cone theorem

A nonzero rank-one prefix matrix gives a first block factor. At each subsequent constrained cut, tensoring by the preceding nonzero factor preserves matrix rank (a left inverse on that factor proves the reverse inequality). Iteration proves the block product property. Conversely the rank of an internal unfolding equals the rank of the relevant block unfolding. This handles redundant cuts and dimension-one modes; zero is separately feasible.

At B₀=λ⊗v_j and a nearby product B, projecting B₀−B off the jth candidate line gives λ sin θ_j≤t. Multiplying the block sine factors gives ||B_S||≤(λ+t)(t/λ)^|S|. This is an inequality for actual feasible tensors, not a Taylor expansion of an assumed smooth TT chart.

The original minimum-degree lower bound is correct: elementary within-block products span every perpendicular subspace, and each path v_j+s w_j has internal TT ranks ≤2. The signal v_j is itself an original-mode product, so these paths need no unexplained tangent-cone approximation. This is the precise use of the rank-one evaluation-point restriction.

The strengthening removes the minimum-degree restriction in the lower bound. For a specified coefficient of degree l, evaluate all 2^l signs of the feasible path and take its top Walsh coefficient. All proper subsets cancel exactly, leaving λ c_S s^l. Therefore some path attains at least that absolute coefficient even when lower-degree loading terms are present. A degree with norm a_l contains at most N_l elementary coordinates, giving |c_S|≥a_l/√N_l. The mean-value inequality s²≥4(t/λ)²/(5l) holds for t≤λ/2, since the derivative of (1+x)^{1/l} is at least 4/(5l) on [0,1/4]. Maximizing over l and the radial path gives a sum lower bound with a fixed dimension constant. This is the key new argument; it is a finite sign identity, not an invocation of novel Fourier theory.

The sharp elementary constant remains a leading local constant only. The positive chart coefficient c≥λ−t and exact orthogonal energy imply c²Σ||z_j||²≤t². AM–GM gives c∏_{j∈S}||z_j||≤t^q/[q^{q/2}c^{q−1}]. The feasible path has the matching limiting coefficient. For q=1, the single-block path gives exactly t. For A=0 the modulus is zero and q is intentionally unused. For t comparable to or greater than λ the sharp local expansion is not asserted.

Slack at every cut is a separate rank-subadditivity argument, not a tangent-space dimension count. It establishes a first-order exponent with a loading-dependent constant and says nothing about ambient efficient variance. The six-mode singularity check correctly uses vanishing differentials of 3×3 minors in a rank≤2 4×4 determinantal variety. Its tangent space has dimension 16 while the variety has dimension 12. The cubic exponent comes from the three external product blocks, not from this internal singularity. Comparisons are restricted to the individual prefix matrix envelopes.

## 2. Information lower bounds and their quantifiers

Conditional Gaussian log density ratios have expectation ||Xδ||²/(2σ²). Since the design law is common, joint relative entropy is δᵀE(S)δ/(2σ²). The isotropic identity is n||δ||²/(2σ²); the extension requires only E(S)≼LnI. No independence between design rows is needed for this identity.

For completeness Pinsker with the convention TV=sup_E|P(E)−Q(E)| is TV≤√(KL/2). A direct verification is: map the two laws to an event attaining or approaching TV; the log-sum inequality bounds their binary relative entropy by KL. For fixed binary reference probability q, the second derivative of D(p||q) in p is 1/[p(1−p)]≥4; D(q||q)=D′(q||q)=0. Integrating twice gives D(p||q)≥2(p−q)². Approximation of the supremizing event gives the claim, including infinite KL by triviality. Log-sum itself follows from Jensen's inequality for −log after normalizing the reference masses. Thus the factor 1/2 multiplying √n||δ||/σ is correct.

Under P₀, each of the events that an interval contains ψ₀ and ψ₁ has probability at least 1−α and 1−α−TV respectively. Their intersection has probability ≥1−2α−TV; on it the length is ≥|ψ₁−ψ₀|. This remains true for singleton intervals, including the fallback. Choosing t=(1−2α)σ/√n leaves bracket at least (1−2α)/2. For arbitrary A, the tube direction is A/||A||, so its target separation is ||A||min(η,t), not merely min(η,t). This factor is now explicit in the loading-uniform theorem.

The tube sandwich is valid for η larger than t as well: use the ambient bound ||A||t, not a Taylor series at t+η. For η≤t, the multiscale polynomial obeys R_A(t+η)≤2^J R_A(t). The lower maximum is at least half the sum. The norm margin in the bounded model keeps the radial alternatives feasible. The unbounded-class corollary replaces only the distance-attainment argument and removes that margin.

Unknown σ does not defeat the lower bound: honesty over all σ includes two alternatives at the same fixed σ. No integration over σ or variance estimation lower bound is needed. The resulting bound is sharp in the powers of n, λ and the displayed loading weights with fixed dimensions, up to constants. It is not a sharp confidence constant, growing-p minimax result, or universal estimation-risk bound.

## 3. Localization, moments, and the exact sample threshold used

For full-rank X and Gaussian ε, S^{1/2}(z−b)/σ is N_p(0,I) conditionally. The squared norm is χ²_p, and the Euclidean error is bounded by that norm divided by √λ_min(S). Orthogonal projection into the column space and its complement makes the residual χ²_{n−p} independent of this vector. Therefore the squared Mahalanobis error divided by p shat_noise² has F_{p,n−p} law. This proves simultaneous ball coverage, not equality of Euclidean-ball coverage; the ball is generally conservative.

With Gaussian design, residualizing a design column against the other p−1 columns leaves ν=n−p+1 independent Gaussian degrees of freedom. Its squared norm is χ²_ν, the reciprocal of a diagonal entry of S⁻¹. Integrating the chi-square density gives E(χ²_ν)^{−k/2}=2^{−k/2}Γ((ν−k)/2)/Γ(ν/2), finite precisely when ν>k. For a≥1 use (Σx_j)^a≤p^{a−1}Σx_j^a; for a=1/2 use concavity plus E tr(S⁻¹)=p/(n−p−1). Since λ_min(S)⁻¹≤tr(S⁻¹), these bounds prove the required negative moments.

The original n≥2p+2J+8 was sufficient but needlessly strong. With m=max(J,2), n≥p+m gives ν≥m+1>k for every required integer k≤m, and n/(n−p−1)≤(p+m)/(m−1). The gamma ratio after multiplication by n^{k/2} is uniformly bounded over these integers by its finite initial segment and asymptotics. No constant is dimension free. This establishes the revised condition; it is not asserted to be the minimal n needed by the interval. Inverse-Gram moments can genuinely fail even at full rank (C8).

Positive moments of residual scale are bounded over n−p≥1. Its conditional distribution is constant in X, which proves independence from X; independence has not been inferred merely from zero correlation. The upper F quantile is uniformly bounded over these integer residual degrees of freedom because it converges to a finite chi-square quantile and the remaining finite set has finite quantiles. These arguments justify actual expectation bounds rather than O_P surrogates.

## 4. Pilot, certificate, and no circular proof dependency

The HOSVD projection residual is bounded by a telescoping sum of commuting mode projections. The terms are mutually orthogonal: for j<k, term j lies off the jth pilot line and term k lies in that line. Each mode residual is no larger than ||z−B₀|| because the true block line is a competitor. Thus residual ≤√J||z−B₀||, and h≤(1+√J)||z−B₀||. No nonconvex convergence theorem or oracle initialization is used.

For h<λ/2, projection off a pilot line gives sin θ_j≤h/λ. Mapping a true degree-l mask into a pilot degree-r mask, r<l, requires at least l−r cross-line maps, each with operator norm at most h/λ. All remaining factors are contractions. Summing finitely many masks proves the mixing bound, with constants depending on J only. This controls lower-order components generated by estimating factors.

The improved deterministic certificate retains c²Σ||z_j||²≤ρ² rather than bounding every block separately. The elementary-symmetric inequality e_l(x)≤C(J,l)(Σx_j/J)^l is proved by averaging unequal coordinate pairs at a maximizer. This replaces the original coefficient √C(J,l) by √C(J,l)/J^{l/2}. Both certificates are valid, and nesting of the structured intervals proves the improved length is no greater on every dataset. Neither this inequality nor the whole confidence radius is claimed to be a sharp expected-length constant.

Because the common ball event does not depend on A, the deterministic argument holds simultaneously for every A. This proves coverage for measurable data-selected loadings and their random targets; it is not conditional selective coverage. Expected length for arbitrary dependent loadings involves E[a_l(Ahat)U^{d_l}], which does not factor without an additional argument. Independent loadings can be conditioned on if the statistical experiment conditional on them is unchanged.

The dependency graph separates deterministic coverage (D6) from uniform expected length. D6 uses nearest-point existence, block factorization, and the certificate only. The transfer theorem's proof reuses that deterministic argument and independently decomposes the loading degrees. It does not assume the expected-length conclusion of the procedure theorem. The procedure's uniform bound then follows from transfer plus OLS moments. Finally the length theorem combines the procedure upper bound with the two-point lower bound. There is no circular reliance on a theorem's own statistical conclusion.

On G={T+η≤c_Jλ}, the graded expression is subadditive in A; the MINIMUM defining H need not be, and the proof does not assert it is. Decompose A into true degrees and apply the mixing estimate to each degree at least two. For degree zero/one, bound every projected norm by a_l and the finite graded sum by C_J a_l rho, using rho/(shat−rho)≤1/6. This bounds the graded expression itself, not merely its minimum. Together these steps bound each summand by a_l(T+η)^{max(1,l)}/λ^{max(1,l)−1}. On Gᶜ use H≤||A||ρ and U1_{Gᶜ}≤U^d/(c_Jλ)^{d−1}. With moments only through m, truncate each degree to min(max(1,l),m). This is the important uniform-integrability check. For η>r_n use ambient intersection immediately. These calculations prove both original and energy procedures, including failure events.

## 5. New weaker-assumption results and what they do not say

The confidence-ball transfer theorem contains no Gaussian or independence assumption by itself. It requires a valid ball for coverage and explicit moments for length. Substitution of a pointwise/asymptotic ball without uniform coverage would not preserve honesty.

For arbitrary design laws with Gaussian conditional errors, inverse-Gram moment bounds give the OLS moments; E S≼LnI gives the information bound. The same rates follow. Gaussian row covariance Σ with bounded eigenvalues is an actual verified example, via S=Σ^{1/2}WΣ^{1/2}. Deterministic well-conditioned Gram matrices are another. No theorem that all heavy-tailed random designs satisfy these moment assumptions has been imported.

Under E(ε|X)=0 and Cov(ε|X)≼σbar²I, with a known σbar, OLS has conditional covariance ≤σbar²S⁻¹. Markov gives the new variance-bound confidence radius. Two localization moments follow from the trace assumption; the guaranteed degree is capped at two. This is a deliberate weaker conclusion. It is not a proof that the Gaussian fast rate is impossible with finite variance, and Section 7 now constructs such a robust localizer for independent pairs with a known conditional variance bound. It is not Huber contamination: an unrestricted contaminating law need not satisfy the conditional mean or covariance bounds. Unknown variance under weak tails is unresolved here.

## 6. Supporting asymptotic and computational statements

The Wald proposition fixes λ,σ,c and p. S/n→I and conditional characteristic functions give the coefficient-independent OLS limit along the triangular alternatives. Each HOSVD covariance has leading eigengap λ², with perturbation O_P(λt_n+t_n²). Project the eigenvector equation into e₀⊥ and invert the separated block; this yields h=(t_n/λ)Q g+O_P(t_n²/λ²). Normalizing the first coordinate and multiplying the three designated coordinates gives the product limit. Tangent projection has three orthogonal leading terms of order (t_n/λ)²; the radial term is smaller. The joint SE limit follows from nS⁻¹→I. All λ factors are valid because λ is fixed in this proposition; it does not cover arbitrary λ_n or c_n.

The denominator in the limiting Wald ratio is positive almost surely. The equality boundary is the zero set of a nonzero polynomial, with zero Gaussian probability (induction on dimension verifies this). Pillai–Meng applies only at c=0 to the monomial with three positive unit exponents and identity covariance. Its χ²₁/9 law gives the z/3 calibration. For c→∞ after n→∞, division by c² gives a standard normal ratio and coverage 2Φ(z/3)−1. Interchanging the limits or treating the calibration as locally honest is unjustified.

The cubic leading coefficient for a full-normal three-block loading follows directly from the injective norm and AM–GM, with maximizing directions existing on compact unit spheres. Arbitrary such block directions are feasible in the stated three-block product class; they would not automatically be feasible under additional internal caps, which the proposition does not impose. The hardness statement concerns evaluating this coefficient as dimensions vary. An exact-evaluation oracle supplies the tensor spectral norm (multiply by 3√3, or square and multiply by 27). It inherits an existing problem, not a new inference-complexity separation or constant-factor inapproximability result.

## Referee conclusions

No counterexample to a baseline numbered theorem was found under its actual assumptions. Four limitations justified proved changes: nonuniform loading constants, an unnecessarily strong n condition, avoidable certificate constants, and unnecessarily specialized localization assumptions. Full fast rates under arbitrary dependence, growing-dimensional minimax constants, general higher-rank evaluation points, and a computational-statistical gap are not established. The independent known-variance-bound finite-variance case is solved in Section 7 below. The paper remains limited even after correcting its presentation of these distinctions.

## 7. Further strengthening completed during the audit

R17 establishes the full finite-variance rate for independent observation pairs and a **known conditional variance upper bound**. Dependence and an unrestricted unknown bound remain separate. For g=2h−1 independent OLS groups, a coordinate group variance is at most 2 sigma_bar²/k. At least h bad groups are required to move the median outside a coordinate interval. Union over h-subsets and p coordinates yields P(||zmed−b||>u)≤min(1,(D/u)^{2h}). Integrating this explicit tail proves moments through degree J when 2h>J, uniformly over the noise laws. The transfer theorem then gives the full contact rate, and its Gaussian submodel supplies a matching inf-interval/sup-noise lower bound. This is a complete proved extension, not merely a suggested robust estimator.

The crude coordinate union bound has a dimension cost; no sharp dimension dependence is claimed. It is acceptable for the fixed-p theorem, and stronger vector-mean theory is acknowledged as prior art. Crucially, observation moments of order J are not assumed. It is the estimator's localization error that has these moments after independent-group median amplification.

C12 justifies the known-bound restriction in this broad conditional-noise model. Conditional mixing with probability w_epsilon(x)=min(1,epsilon[1+(x^T delta)²]) produces exact regression mean x^T b1, a uniform conditional variance bound sigma0²+1/epsilon, and n-observation TV≤n epsilon(1+||delta||²). Letting epsilon→0 at fixed n forces baseline coverage of every target in the parameter range. Tonelli gives expected length at least (1−alpha) times that range, or infinity for an unbounded range. This proof is explicit and does not import the unavailable original Bahadur–Savage full text. It concerns uniformity over all conditional variance bounds and permits conditional heteroskedasticity; it does not invalidate Gaussian unknown-sigma inference.

C8 additionally separates sufficient inverse moments from universal necessity: a different procedure can sometimes trim poorly conditioned designs. The explicit deterministic full-rank design X_i=1/n has S=1/n and gives vanishing TV for fixed coefficient shifts, so full rank alone does not imply any shrinking root-n honest-length guarantee. This information example, rather than infinite expectation of one radius alone, justifies the broader negative extension claim.
