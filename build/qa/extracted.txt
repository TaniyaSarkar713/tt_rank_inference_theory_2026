Contact Orders and the Fragility of Honest Tensor Inference
Research draft for author review
7 September 2026
Abstract
A linear functional of a tensor coefficient can have a vanishing first-order derivative when
restricted to a rank-constrained model. We study the resulting inference problem in Gaussian
tensor regression with fixed ambient dimension. At an original-mode rank-one signal, we classify
the local modulus for every tensor-train rank-cap profile: cuts constrained to rank one partition
the modes into blocks, and the first nonzero block degree of the loading determines the contact
order q. The optimal pointwise expected length among intervals honest over the entire class
is of order (σ/√n)q/λq−1, where λ is signal magnitude. The classification includes examples
with singular internal rank constraints. In a known Frobenius neighborhood of radius η, the
optimal order acquires the term η ∧(σ/√n). We construct a computable interval using a
Gaussian confidence ball, an HOSVD pilot, and all block orders, with an exact extension to
unknown variance. Simulations recover the predicted exponents and distinguish exact-boundary
Wald calibration from locally honest inference, while documenting substantial conservatism
of the proposed interval. The contribution is a tensor-specific classification and sensitivity
calculation; generic higher-order inference, modulus lower bounds, and tensor tangent geometry
are established tools.
Keywords: confidence intervals; tensor train; local modulus; singular inference; model approxima-
tion; higher-order contact.

Introduction
Structural restrictions can reduce uncertainty about a regression functional by more than a variance
constant. Consider a four-way coefficient B ∈R2×2×2×2 whose TT rank caps are (1, 2, 1). The outer
rank-one cuts force a product of blocks of dimensions (2, 4, 2). At B0 = λE0000, the coordinate
functional B 7→B1111 changes only when all three blocks change. A perturbation of Frobenius
size t can change this functional by order t3/λ2. This raises a statistical question: can an interval
exploit that cubic behavior while retaining coverage over the whole rank-constrained class, including
neighboring rank strata?
Existing theory makes a simple higher-order calculation insufficient as a contribution. Functional
inference under degeneracy, including corrected bootstrap and minimum-distance methods, is well
developed (Chen and Fang, 2019; Bei and Navjeevan, 2026). Tensor inference with nondegenerate
tangent alignment also has strong guarantees (Xu et al., 2025; Ma and Xia, 2024). What requires a
separate calculation here is the local modulus across a specified collection of TT constraints and the
corresponding expected length under global honesty. In addition, structural rank restrictions may
be approximate, and a small coefficient departure can be larger than a higher-order inferential scale.
We therefore study a deliberately explicit experiment: independent isotropic Gaussian designs,
independent Gaussian regression errors, and a fixed-dimensional coefficient in a bounded TT rank
class or a known neighborhood of that class. The estimand is a fixed linear functional of the
1


coefficient, not an individual TT core, a training-trajectory statistic, or an unobserved neural-
network parameterization. Our main objective is to classify the best expected interval length
at rank-one coefficients subject to coverage for every coefficient in the declared class. We allow
unknown error variance but do not claim inference when ambient least squares is unavailable.
Three obstacles distinguish the calculation from a direct delta-method exercise. First, a path in
an enclosing product model may violate an internal TT rank cap, so a lower modulus bound requires
an actually feasible tensor path. Second, a confidence interval calibrated at the exact boundary
can fail under statistically indistinguishable local changes, as Figure 1 illustrates. Third, the fitted
loading projections have lower-order components even when the true ones vanish; these terms and
the event of poor pilot localization must be controlled to bound expected length.
The construction uses the cuts whose rank cap equals one to partition the tensor chain. Or-
thogonal loading components are graded by the number of blocks perpendicular to the signal. The
smallest nonzero degree determines the modulus exponent. Within-block sums of two elementary
products provide feasible witness paths despite the remaining TT constraints. Confidence-ball
inversion then supplies global coverage, while a graded product bound makes the calculation explicit.
In the four-way example this recovers cubic inference. A six-way example with a proper singular
internal rank constraint has the same cubic order, demonstrating both the scope of the classification
and the limited role of that singularity.
The paper makes three claims, each proved in the appendix:
1. Section 4 classifies local contact and optimal honest expected length at rank-one signals for
arbitrary positive TT cap profiles. It also shows that slack at every cut restores first-order
contact for every nonzero loading.
2. Theorem 3 determines the additional length required by a known structural approximation
allowance and identifies departures that cannot be detected but invalidate exact-model lengths.
3. Section 5 gives a computable, globally honest interval that attains these orders without
selecting the contact order. The experiments test the exponents, local coverage distinction,
and approximation sensitivity.

Related work and the scope of the contribution
Degenerate functionals and singular tests.
Chen and Fang (2019) develops second-order
functional inference and explains ordinary-bootstrap failure; higher-order extensions are explicitly
discussed. Dufour et al. (2025) studies locally singular polynomial Wald restrictions. Pillai and
Meng (2016) gives the exact Gaussian monomial Wald law, including the cubic χ2
1/9 case. Bei and
Navjeevan (2026) constructs uniformly valid minimum-distance inference under degeneracy and
discusses extensions beyond a nonsingular Hessian. These results preclude novelty claims based
solely on a cubic delta method or a nonstandard Wald limit. Our explicit local Wald calculation is
a supporting specialization of that literature.
Moduli, curved models, and model neighborhoods.
The connection between honest interval
length and between-class moduli is central to Cai and Low (2004). Its lower-bound logic applies
to singleton evaluation classes and does not require a new tensor information inequality. Andrews
and Mikusheva (2016) already obtains conservative inference from curved-model geometry. Model-
misspecification sensitivity is studied by Bonhomme and Weidner (2022), and bias-aware intervals
and efficiency comparisons by Armstrong and Koles´ar (2021); Armstrong et al. (2023).
Our
neighborhood calculation uses the elementary modulus sandwich; the specific input is the classified
TT contact order. We do not claim a new general theory of robust confidence intervals.

Rank uncertainty and tensor inference.
Carpentier et al. (2018) shows that rank-adaptive
matrix confidence sets can exist, with feasibility depending on the observation and noise experiment.
Armstrong et al. (2026) allows weak factors in panel inference. Thus rank uncertainty is not a
universal impossibility mechanism. In regular tensor settings, Xu et al. (2025) and Ma and Xia
(2024) obtain functional Gaussian limits under signal, initialization, and nondegenerate alignment
conditions. Their dimensions can increase, and their regular-regime results are stronger than the
fixed-dimensional analysis here in that respect. We address a different boundary regime rather than
improving their sample complexity. Xia et al. (2022) also establishes important tensor inference
results without a universal need for debiasing.
Tensor geometry, estimation, and computation.
Kutschan (2018) characterizes TT tangent
cones, and Yang et al. (2025) gives first- and second-order variational formulas for determinantal
tensor formats. Higher block-degree spans for product varieties appear in the osculating geometry of
Araujo et al. (2019). We use that grading and verify feasible lower-bound paths inside the additional
TT constraints. TT regression estimation and algorithmic guarantees are already supplied by Qin
and Zhu (2025); oracle tensor regression under separation is studied by Wang et al. (2025). The
spectral-norm hardness observation in Proposition 5 inherits the result of Hillar and Lim (2013); it
is not an independent complexity theorem.
Modern tensor models and other inferential targets.
Tensor regression networks (Kossaifi
et al., 2020), global rank-adaptive TT adapters (Lopez-Piqueres et al., 2026), and statistical rank

allocation for LoRA (Gao and Tan, 2026) motivate attention to rank restrictions. Their architectural

conformal completion (Sun and Chen, 2026) and the recent dual-channel tensor framework (Chen
et al., 2026) further demonstrate that tensor uncertainty quantification is already an active area.
Predictive intervals and structure-comparison guarantees in those experiments do not determine the
honest length studied here.

Discussion and limitations
The main conclusion is a classification of functional contact at original-mode rank-one points in
capped TT classes. Cap-one cuts determine the exponent, while internal constraints can leave a
singular feasible set without changing that exponent. If every cut permits an additional rank, all
nonzero linear functionals return to first order. These statements describe the geometry of the
declared class, not a universal benefit of tensor parameterization or rank selection.
The approximation result is consequential for interpretation. A confidence interval with an
n−3/2 pointwise length relies on a structural allowance of the same order if that gain is to persist
uniformly. An ambient coefficient departure of order between n−3/2 and n−1/2 can be statistically
undetectable and still dominate the target uncertainty. The theorem therefore argues for explicit
sensitivity analysis whenever rank restrictions are approximate. It does not provide a way to infer
the required small allowance from the same sample without additional assumptions.
The limitations are substantial. The pointwise length theorem fixes ambient dimension and
the signal directions, with an original-mode rank-one evaluation point. The OLS ball can incur
large dimension constants, and the experiments show that the computable interval is far from a
sharp constant procedure. No matching dimension-dependent minimax length, efficient confidence
constant, or general classification at non-rank-one TT points has been established. The inherited
12


hardness of an exact local coefficient does not establish hardness of statistically optimal inference.
Status of possible extensions.
Partial: growing-dimensional inference has a valid ambient
localization construction and fixed-geometry lower bounds, but their dimension factors do not
match. Conjectured: a general classification at non-rank-one points with several weak bonds may
require higher-order feasible arcs beyond the present elementary paths; no universal rate is asserted.
Partial: the spectral-norm reduction identifies a difficult constant but leaves computationally
optimal intervals unresolved. Conjectured: non-Gaussian or dependent-noise versions would need
new honest localization and moment bounds. These statements are research questions, not theorems
of this paper.
7.1
Relevance to modern AI and why no LLM benchmark is claimed
A tensor regression head on frozen features offers a direct statistical setting for coefficient functionals.
In a locally linear adapter model, a fixed evaluation contrast has the form ⟨A, B⟩, with A determined
by held-out or externally fixed Jacobian features. Existing TT adapters make such restrictions
plausible modeling choices, but a trained LLM does not automatically satisfy isotropic design,
Gaussian residuals, fixed dimensions, or a known approximation allowance.
A fully observed
checkpoint is also a deterministic approximation object, not the sampling experiment in (1).
Accordingly, this draft contains no real-data or trained-LLM result. The companion application
protocol specifies a frozen-feature regression study with a separately fixed functional, sample splitting
for any learned loading, residual checks, and an explicit sensitivity grid. A convincing application
would need to justify the population target and structural approximation bound, rather than add an
LLM benchmark to support a general mathematical claim. The present theory applies independently
of any AI interpretation.
Research assessment.
The completed fixed-dimensional results support a focused theoretical
manuscript for further expert evaluation. The central novelty candidate is the cap-profile classification
and its inferential interpretation, not generic higher-order asymptotics. A broader theory of local
moduli at singular structured parameters, or dimension-dependent optimality with a useful procedure,
would be needed to make a strong case for the Annals of Statistics. The current draft should not be
treated as submission ready solely because its proofs and experiments are complete.
Reproducibility and proof status.

theorem ledger, assumption audit, canonical proof source, fixed-seed simulation code, replicate

A.1
Product structure at cap-one cuts
Lemma 8. Every nonzero tensor obeying the cap-one prefix constraints is a product across the
induced consecutive blocks. Conversely, if a product of block tensors has within-block TT ranks at
most the corresponding internal caps, it belongs to the full capped TT class whenever its norm is at
most M.
Proof. At the first cap-one cut, a nonzero rank-one unfolding factors as a vector for the first block
times a vector for the remaining modes. A later prefix unfolding is, up to row ordering, the
Kronecker product of the first vector and the corresponding prefix unfolding of the remaining tensor.
Multiplication by a nonzero vector preserves rank. The next cap-one constraint therefore factors the
remaining tensor again. Induction proves the first assertion. The same rank-preservation calculation
shows that an internal unfolding of a block product has the rank of that block’s internal unfolding; a
block boundary has rank one. This proves the converse. Zero belongs to both classes separately.
