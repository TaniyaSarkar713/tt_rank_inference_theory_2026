# Supplied corpus: 18 PDFs, 519 pages

Retained from the 6 September 2026 source review. Its mathematical descriptions inform this reassessment; its earlier direction-ranking is superseded by literature_review.md. This is a substantive review, not certification of every original proof.


The identifiers P01–P18 refer to the alphabetical file inventory, not to a quality ranking. Page references are PDF page numbers unless otherwise stated. A file concordance appears at the end of the report.

## Statistical theory, optimization, and neural-network foundations

**P02 — Qin and Zhu, Computational and Statistical Guarantees for Tensor-on-Tensor Regression with Tensor Train Decomposition.** The supplied version is arXiv:2406.06002v2, 1 May 2025, 21 pages. The model is tensor-valued linear regression with a TT coefficient tensor. Section II gives restricted-isometry-based recovery bounds and a minimax lower bound; Section III analyzes TT-SVD iterative hard thresholding and factorized Riemannian gradient descent with initialization conditions. This is a useful starting point for TT estimation and its computational costs. Crucially, the efficient factorized method has a worse stated error bound than IHT, including extra order/rank/conditioning factors. The paper does not license the claim that every TT algorithm automatically attains the intrinsic statistical rate. Nor does it establish uniform inference after data-driven rank selection or justify RIP for LLM activations. **Use:** estimation baselines and TT perturbation tools for Direction 1. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06002).

**P04 — Wang, Shi, and Zhao, High-Dimensional Tensor Regression with Oracle Properties.** ICML 2025, 41 pages. The framework uses nonconvex penalties for sparsity and low-dimensional tensor subspaces. Theorems 5–6 in the main text require curvature conditions and minimum signal/singular-value conditions for oracle-type error rates. Assumption 4 permits a jointly Gaussian design with controlled covariance; it should not be described simply as an independent-isotropic-design result. The statistical contribution is stronger than a parameter-count argument, but oracle risk is not uniform post-selection coverage. A nominally small nonzero singular value can violate the signal condition precisely where adaptive-rank uncertainty becomes interesting. **Use:** a direct comparator for regular versus weak-signal regimes; adding SCAD/MCP to a tensor regression problem is already established. [ICML paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wang25co.html).

**P06 — Xia, Ng, and Zhang, Low-Rank Tensor Learning by Generalized Nonconvex Regularization.** Supplied preprint dated October 2024, 52 pages. It penalizes singular values of frontal slices after a unitary transform, with a differentiable loss and an entrywise boundedness constraint. Theorem 2 gives a stationary-point error bound under RSC and penalty regularity. Theorems 3–4 establish convergence of proximal majorization-minimization to a stationary point and rates conditional on a Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz exponent. **TTNN here means transformed tensor nuclear norm; it is not tensor-train rank.** “Global convergence” of the sequence must not be confused with convergence to a global minimizer. Transformed-slice geometry also depends on the chosen transform. **Use:** nonconvex estimation tools, but re-establish design and curvature assumptions for any new application. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18402).

**P14 — Auddy, Xia, and Yuan, Tensor Methods in High Dimensional Data Analysis: Opportunities and Challenges.** Supplied May 2024 survey, 38 pages. It organizes several statistical tensor models and stresses noise sensitivity, statistical–computational gaps, and the differences between tensor and matrix problems. Its discussions of tensor SVD, regression, completion, and factor models provide the statistical baseline that an AI-oriented paper must surpass. It already cites optimal tensor estimation, tensor inference, and provable TT completion. The right lesson is to specify the observation model, structural class, loss, and algorithmic regime before claiming an advantage. **Use:** the most valuable broad statistical entry point in the folder; follow its references into original results. [Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18412).

**P03 — Srinivas, Arvanitakis, and Vijayaraghavan, Guarantees for Alternating Least Squares in Overparameterized Tensor Decomposition.** The supplied file is an 18-slide 2025 workshop presentation, rather than a full paper. I located the corresponding NeurIPS 2025 paper by Arvanitakis, Srinivas, and Vijayaraghavan. Its analysis concerns a parallel-update ALS variant with approximately quadratic overparameterization in the true CP rank, random initialization, and dimensional/conditioning requirements. It also studies low-rank approximation. Exact reconstruction with extra components does not mean recovery of the original identifiable factors. A deterministic approximation guarantee in terms of the full noise norm is not automatically a minimax denoising guarantee. **Use:** the central algorithmic starting point for Direction 3. The parallel and usual sequential ALS algorithms must be distinguished. [Full NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/70791f20a907b7f7cb2ad8ade32486b2-Paper-Conference.pdf).

**P05 — Borsoi, Usevich, and Clausel, Low-Rank Tensor Decompositions for the Theory of Neural Networks.** Supplied December 2025 version, 21 pages. This review connects tensor formats with expressivity, learnability, identifiability, and computational hardness. Sections on polynomial and sum-product networks give exact tensor representations; other neural architectures require approximations or more specialized constructions. CP uniqueness does not transfer verbatim to Tucker factors or TT cores, which have gauge symmetries. Nor do polynomial-network results establish a theorem for an unrestricted deep softmax transformer. **Use:** a rigorous bridge for an AI-motivated statistical project, particularly Direction 3, and a safeguard against claiming that arbitrary neural networks are simply low-rank tensors. [Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18408).

**P18 — Tadipatri, Haeffele, Agterberg, and Vidal, A Convex Relaxation Approach to Generalization Analysis for Parallel Positively Homogeneous Networks.** AISTATS 2025, 70 pages. The framework relates a nonconvex parameterization to a convex optimization problem over functions. Its generalization bound separates statistical error and a residual optimization term; the main theorem assumes boundedness/Lipschitz and distributional conditions, with extensions in the appendix. The statistical term explicitly involves fitted width and component dimension. The attention specialization uses a particular single-layer model; it is not a blanket generalization theorem for full LLMs. **Use:** a serious competing foundation for any claim about generalization of tensorized networks. Direction 3 must obtain an algorithm-specific intrinsic-rank result beyond merely instantiating this framework. [AISTATS paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v258/tadipatri25a.html).

## Tensor adapters, compression, and pretraining

**P07 — Lopez-Piqueres and colleagues, MetaTT: A Global Tensor-Train Adapter for Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning.** Supplied arXiv v3, 2 July 2026, 37 pages. The adapter stacks input/output dimensions, layer, matrix type, and optionally heads/tasks into one TT representation. Section 2 makes the model explicit; later sections study multitask adaptation and a DMRG-inspired rank-adaptive optimizer. Thus “global TT adapter,” “task as another mode,” and “adaptive TT ranks for fine-tuning” are already taken. The contribution is largely architecture/optimization and empirical parameter efficiency, not a sampling-distribution theory for downstream effects. The review must use v3, because an older abstract understates its multitask and rank-adaptive content. **Use:** the most direct implementation baseline for Direction 1. [Current paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09105).

**P12 — Ye, Zhang, and Zhang, TRAC: Tensor-Train Based Across-Layer Compression for Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning.** ICLR 2026, 34 pages. TRAC combines tensor cores, freezing/sharing, and lightweight controllers that retain layer-specific flexibility. Experiments span language and vision architectures. This substantially overlaps any proposed contribution framed only as exploiting across-layer redundancy with tensor trains. Reported means and standard deviations characterize experimental variation; they do not constitute inferential guarantees for population functionals. The sharing assumption can also introduce bias when layers or tasks have incompatible update geometry. **Use:** compare against MetaTT and TRAC, including strong individual adapters, when evaluating an inference or statistical adaptation method. [Official ICLR record](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10006929).

**P11 — Zhang and colleagues, TEON: Tensorized Orthonormalization Beyond Layer-Wise MUON for Large Language Model Pre-Training.** Supplied February 2026 version, 19 pages. TEON stacks gradients across layers and orthogonalizes a matricization. Section 4 compares norm-based convergence guarantees and explains when aligned leading singular vectors offer a benefit; the best-case improvement is conditional, not universal. The result concerns optimization stationarity under specified smoothness/noise assumptions, not minimax estimation, valid confidence intervals, or unconditional improvement in held-out risk. Gradient tensors change through training and are not the same object as a low-rank weight tensor. **Use:** motivates studying statistical consequences of cross-layer coupling; a new tensor optimizer alone would need a much stronger statistical story for the requested journals. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23261).

**P10 — TENSORSLM: Sub-Billion Parameter Language Model Compression for Low-End Devices Based on Tensor-Train Decomposition.** The supplied 21-page version is anonymous and marked under review at ICLR 2025. Its central operation is TT-SVD of individual token embeddings, with language-task, latency, and energy evaluations. A public related TensorSLM paper by Xu, Xu, and Mandic was posted in June 2025 and lists an ICML workshop, so the local review header should not be cited as proof of ICLR acceptance. The per-token tensorization is distinct from a globally shared TT embedding table. Approximation tolerances alone do not certify rare-token or task-specific performance. **Use:** a lightweight compression baseline. [Public TensorSLM record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13514).

**P09 — Xu, Xu, and Mandic, Surviving Resource-constraint Compression: Capability Retention under Tensor-train Decomposition for Sub-billion Parameter Language Models.** Supplied Machine Intelligence Research article, 16 pages, DOI printed as 10.1007/s11633-026-1653-x. It studies embedding and dense-layer compression and reports different task-retention patterns across model families. These are useful observations, but categorizing benchmark gains as preservation of “reasoning” versus “lexical knowledge” is an interpretation, not an identified causal mechanism. The paper substantially overlaps the TensorSLM line of work, so these two PDFs should not be counted as independent replications. Its model-dependent failures motivate Direction 2. **Use:** hypotheses for stratified experiments, rather than a universal capability-preservation theorem. The DOI endpoint did not resolve through this session's browser; metadata and content here are supported by the supplied PDF.

**P08 — Rethinking the Role of Tensor Decompositions in Post-Training LLM Compression.** Supplied anonymous 12-page manuscript with placeholder conference metadata. It reports degradation across dense/MoE settings and discusses shared-subspace mismatch and residual-stream distortion. Those observations are useful counterevidence to an assumption that tensorization always helps. However, Section 3's operator-norm argument contains claims that fail simple counterexamples; Section 3 of this memorandum explains them. Its publication status and author identity were not established. **Use:** empirical failure cases to reproduce, while avoiding reliance on its claimed general theoretical explanation.

**P17 — Yoon and Kim, Performance Comparison of Tensor Decomposition on LLMs.** International Journal of Fuzzy Logic and Intelligent Systems, June 2025, 10 pages. It compares CP and Tucker decompositions in small transformer experiments on NSMC and Tiny Shakespeare, including rank and memory/time comparisons. The study supplies experimental examples but no minimax theory, inferential procedure, or characterization of when tensor structure is statistically useful. A measured reduction in memory is not guaranteed by a nominal rank comparison alone because implementations and intermediate buffers matter. **Use:** introductory empirical context; it is not the main standard against which to establish a contemporary statistics-journal contribution. [Article DOI](https://doi.org/10.5391/IJFIS.2025.25.2.136).

**P13 — Tarasov, Ahmadi-Asl, de Almeida, and Cichocki, Tensor Methods for Language Models.** Supplied 31 August 2026 survey, 58 pages. Its lifecycle taxonomy, distinction between semantic and imposed tensorization, and discussion of rank selection, tensorization schemes, optimization, and runtime efficiency are directly relevant. Sections 9–10 already identify several broad research directions one might otherwise propose. Its compression-realization metric emphasizes that fewer parameters need not imply faster execution under a comparable benchmark. This is a discovery map rather than proof that a proposed gap is untouched: the primary papers, including September 2026 work, still need checking. **Use:** the central current map of LLM methods and a guide to baselines. [Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30505).

## Attention and tensor regression layers

**P01 — Nakatsuji and colleagues, Tensorized Attention for Understanding Multi-Object Relationships.** AAAI 2025, 9 pages; filename `02835-NakatsujiM.pdf`. TAM adds a semantic object to query/key interactions and uses Tucker-style tensor operations followed by semantic fusion. It evaluates response selection and question answering, including a LoRA-based Llama2 application. Adding structured third-object information is already a concrete architectural contribution. Empirical improvements do not identify a unique semantic tensor factor, prove that ordinary transformers cannot represent such functions, or establish statistical efficiency. **Use:** motivates structured multi-object inputs; a new project would need a population model and an identifiable interaction target. [AAAI article](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/34675).

**P15 — Kossaifi and colleagues, Tensor Regression Networks.** JMLR 2020, 21 pages. Tensor contraction and regression layers retain multilinear structure rather than flattening all activations into a fully connected layer. Section 3.4 explicitly uses a Tucker-structured regression tensor and learns its factors end to end. ImageNet and UK Biobank applications demonstrate the idea's scope. This is an older but essential predecessor: attaching a tensor-regression head to neural features is not itself new. Frozen-feature statistical guarantees and jointly learned-feature guarantees are different claims; uncertainty from learning the feature map cannot be silently discarded. **Use:** architecture and application motivation for Direction 1. [JMLR paper](https://jmlr.org/papers/v21/18-503.html).

**P16 — Klein, Kusch, Sager, Schnake, and Schotthöfer, Tucker Attention: A Generalization of Approximate Attention Mechanisms.** Supplied March/April 2026 preprint, 21 pages. It stacks the composed query-key matrices and composed value-output matrices into tensors, then factorizes them directly. Appendix B establishes rank representations encompassing MHA/GQA/MLA. These are algebraic representation results, not statistical rank-recovery or inference theorems. Its RoPE construction uses a latent-space modification, so compatibility should not be taken to mean every pretrained standard-RoPE model is unchanged by reparameterization. **Use:** the natural tensor objects for Direction 4; the inferential target should be the resulting function or a canonical representation, not arbitrary Q/K factors. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.30033).
