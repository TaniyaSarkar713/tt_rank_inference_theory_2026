# Adversarial novelty review, 9 September 2026

This is a targeted update to the existing 28-paper matrix and the 8 September addendum. Searches included TT/MPS normal geometry, higher-order tangent spaces, rank-boundary inference, local moduli, weak identification, tensor sketching, and conservative Monte Carlo inversion. No exact match to G1 plus C4 was located. Search failure is not proof of novelty, especially across mathematical physics terminology. Results through the accessible literature on 9 September 2026 are considered; the remainder of 2026 is not forecast.

## Novelty verdict

**Candidate contribution with a defensible mathematical boundary:** under per-mode annihilation of a product loading, a general exact-rank TT tensor has local modulus t^K/[K^{K/2}∏βj] with a uniform sharp limit. A general-background weak component gives a complete order transition. In an explicit rank-two experiment, the transition is attained by a globally honest interval using 6K−3 selected features even when p>n, and differs from the quadratic modulus of every single-prefix relaxation.

**Established ingredients:** TT representations, cross interpolation, higher-order MPS variations, Gaussian sketching, F ellipsoids, two-point confidence lower bounds, and confidence-set nuisance maximization. Combining established ingredients does not by itself establish publication-level novelty. A reviewer could regard the aligned case as a cleverly selected product-mean problem; G1 and the explicit comparison with each matrix relaxation are the strongest response currently available.

**Not claimed:** first TT higher-order geometry, optimal confidence constants, a universally superior interval, a necessary dimension penalty for unknown orientations, or a general solution to weak identification.

The remaining strongest novelty target is a sharp theorem for one procedure over many unknown orientations in the regime σ/√n ≪ b ≲ σ√(W/n). Neither an orientation-packing lower bound nor an attaining construction in that regime is yet proved. This is a gap in this project, not an assertion that no external work could resolve it.
